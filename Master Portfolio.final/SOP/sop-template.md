<h3 class="slide-title">Wine Locker Reservation Flow</h3>

<p class="slide-sub">
  I used this concept to show how I think about simplifying a member-facing process.
  In the original version, too much of the reservation flow depended on manual forms and staff follow-up.
  My goal was to make the steps easier to understand and easier to manage on the back end.
</p>

<div class="op-callout">
  <strong>What I was solving</strong>
  <p>
    Reserving a wine locker involved a few different pieces, including availability, policy acknowledgment, and billing.
    I wanted to bring those into one clear flow so the member could complete the request more easily and the team could process it with less manual work.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="hero-frame">
  <div class="story-sequence">
    <article class="story-block">
      <p class="story-label">Problem</p>
      <h4>Too much manual handling</h4>
      <p>
        The process relied on PDFs, extra follow-up, and staff memory, which made it slower and harder to track.
      </p>
    </article>

    <article class="story-block">
      <p class="story-label">Approach</p>
      <h4>Bring everything into one flow</h4>
      <p>
        I organized the experience around three simple actions: choose a locker, review the terms, and submit the request.
      </p>
    </article>

    <article class="story-block">
      <p class="story-label">Outcome</p>
      <h4>Clearer for members and staff</h4>
      <p>
        The result is a cleaner process that is easier to complete on the member side and easier to manage correctly on the staff side.
      </p>
    </article>
  </div>

  <div class="roi-callout">
    <strong>Why I included this</strong>
    <p>
      This example shows how I break down a workflow, identify friction points, and turn a complicated process into something more usable.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
